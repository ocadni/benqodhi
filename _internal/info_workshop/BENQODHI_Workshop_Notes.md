## Overview

**BENQODHI** — BENchmarking Quantum Optimization: from Datasets to Health Innovation

Expert workshop on quantum computing and optimization for life sciences and health.

- **Dates:** 10–11 September 2026
- **Venue:** JRC Ispra, Italy
- **Organising unit:** JRC.F.7, within the BENQODHI exploratory activity granted by JRC.S.4
- **Contact:** JRC-F7-CONFERENCES@ec.europa.eu

**Theme:** Can quantum computing make a measurable difference for optimization problems in health and biology? The workshop brings together experts in quantum computing, optimisation, AI, biology and health to define credible, open benchmarks for transparent comparison across quantum, hybrid, AI and classical approaches.

**Structure:** Two days — Foundations & Use Cases · Benchmark Design · JRC Quantum Laboratories · European Strategy.

**Focus areas:**
1. **Focus** — Make the questions real: optimization challenges rooted in health and biology.
2. **Method** — Make comparisons fair: instances, baselines, metrics and resource accounting.
3. **Outcome** — Make evidence reusable: a practical pathway toward open benchmark resources.

**Contributors:** Researchers from institutions including Sapienza University of Rome, Leiden University, IBM Research Europe (Zurich), ICREA/Universitat Pompeu Fabra, SISSA, PacBio, IRCCS Ospedale San Raffaele, Barcelona Supercomputing Centre, CINECA and the University of Cagliari. Representatives from DG CNECT, DG SANTE, DG DEFIS and the EuroHPC Joint Undertaking are also invited to contribute.

## Day 2 Morning — Parallel Working Tables

*Internal preparation note, working draft. The four-table structure is provisional; scope, contributor mix, facilitation model and use of AI support will be refined before the workshop.*

### Purpose

Turn the Day 1 exchanges into a small number of concrete, reusable next steps. Objective is to develop concrete candidates and a usable common framework, not to survey all possible applications.

### Target outcome

Three focused candidate benchmark dossiers, plus a shared framework note for instances, baselines, reporting and repository needs. Each output should identify the key unresolved choice and a named next action.

### Working tables

| Table | Focus and preparation question | Target output |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Large-scale bioinformatics and genomics optimization (incl. NP-hard) | Problems whose principal difficulty comes from massive, high-dimensional or otherwise demanding biological/genomic data (optimisation need not be combinatorially hard) — e.g. DNA/RNA similarity or matching measures, metabolic-network problems, related biological-network optimisation. Define a concrete use case, data representation and scale, classical/AI baselines, and what a realistic benchmark should test. Quantum applications may be more distant here and should not be assumed. | Candidate dossier: data scale, task/formulation, baselines and measurable outcome |
| 2. AI / QML | — | Candidate dossier: formal formulation, instances, hardness and comparison plan |
| 3. Radiotherapy and other hard health problems, medical imaging | Radiotherapy planning is the starting point. Other health problems included only where they offer a concrete hard or potentially NP-hard formulation. Healthcare resource allocation is not assumed at this stage. | Candidate dossier, or scoped follow-up plan for a well-defined problem |
| 4. Benchmark framework: instances, baselines, repository and reporting | Cross-cutting framework to support the scientific tables: candidate instances/datasets and generators; baseline algorithms; metrics; resource accounting; metadata, provenance and reproducibility; repository structure; expected benchmark outputs and governance | Minimum viable framework note aligned with the three dossiers |

### Roles

Facilitators do not need to be subject experts — their main role is to keep discussion on time, focused on the agreed output, and inclusive. Subject expertise is a bonus, and contributors may also join a table of interest.

| Role | Core function |
|---|---|
| Facilitator | Keeps discussion focused on the agreed output, protects time, ensures constructive and cross-disciplinary contributions |
| Rapporteur | Fills in the output template, prepares the short recap, records open choices, captures the named next action |
| Scientific contributors | Bring domain, optimisation, quantum, data or infrastructure expertise; test assumptions and identify what's needed for a credible benchmark |
| Organisers | Provide templates, help resolve dependencies between tables, lead plenary consolidation |
| AI support | To be designed after the preparatory meeting — a dedicated helper at every table is not assumed at this stage |

A pool of potential facilitators, rapporteurs and scientific contributors (drawn from the invited institutions and JRC staff) is being assembled separately; allocation to tables will be confirmed once attendance and interests are known.

### Common output and open questions for the preparatory meeting

Each scientific table should leave with a compact record of: the use case; formal problem; data/instance provenance and scale; source of computational difficulty; candidate baselines; intended measures of success; what is ready or missing; and the next action. Table 4 should translate these needs into a common benchmark framework.

Open questions:
- Confirm the four-table structure is feasible; if not, merge Tables 1 and 2 into a single bioinformatics and biological-networks table.
- Select one concrete candidate problem or design question per scientific table, avoiding broad background discussion.
- Identify tentative facilitators, rapporteurs and scientific contributors for each table.
- Agree how AI support should be used: a person at each table, shared support, optional tools for facilitators/rapporteurs, or post-session consolidation only.
- Agree the output template and the links between the three scientific dossiers and Table 4.

### Definition of success

A scoped working-table design, named process roles, candidate concrete problems, and a shared route from the Day 2 outputs to the initial BENQODHI benchmark framework.

## Day 2 Afternoon — Policy Session

*Planning note, working draft.*

### Purpose

Close the workshop by connecting the day's benchmarking work to European policy: test what infrastructure, evidence and timelines policymakers actually need, and turn technical outputs into a short set of policy-relevant takeaways with named follow-up.

### Format and timing

14:30–16:00, Day 2 afternoon. Structure and timings below are a proposal (see afternoon table above for the current slot breakdown).

### Round-table design

The round table is the core of the session: a brief framing intervention from each perspective (3–4 minutes, no slides), followed by a moderated discussion across the guiding questions below.

Suggested panel: DG CNECT, DG SANTE, DG DEFIS and DG RTD, plus one scientific voice from the workshop, with organisers moderating. The DG SANTE and DG DEFIS representatives are expected to give short interventions rather than full talks, reflecting their tentative and secondhand confirmation status.

### Guiding questions

1. Where can rigorous benchmarks genuinely inform European decisions on quantum and hybrid computing for health and biology — and where are claims still premature?
2. What infrastructure and access (EuroHPC, QPUs, curated data) do European teams need to run and reproduce these benchmarks?
3. What evidence and reporting would make results trustworthy for policy and procurement?
4. What are realistic timelines for impact in health and biology, and which European instruments (funding, standards, open datasets) could carry the workshop's outputs forward?

### Expected outputs

- A short list of policy-relevant takeaways: where benchmarking can and cannot inform decisions, and the main infrastructure/evidence gaps.
- A first view of which European instruments could carry the outputs forward.
- A named owner for each follow-up.

These feed directly into the concrete-outputs and closing slot.

### Open items to confirm

- Infrastructure and access speaker — EuroHPC JU / CINECA.
- Research and innovation contact and slot — DG to be confirmed (assumed DG RTD).
- Round-table moderator — one of the organisers.
- Short interventions and round-table seats for the DG SANTE and DG DEFIS representatives.
